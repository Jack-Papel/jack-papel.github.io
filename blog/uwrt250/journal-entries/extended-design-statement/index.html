Craft­ing Access­ible & Inclus­ive Multi­modal Texts

Preface

For a short in-class activity, two of my classmates, Alex and Lance, and I were tasked with creating accessibility guidelines for our blogs. We researched various accessibility guidelines for creating inclusive websites, and came up with this synthesized list of traits we thought were most important.

Extended design statement

Our blogs should be...

Percievable: that is, it needs to meet various criteria that guarantee people with all sorts of visual issues can view and understand it; fonts should be legible; headings should stand out

Navigable: not everyone has a mouse, for example, or can see the screen, so our sites should be navigable by other means such as tab navigation; external resources (or internal references) should have links

Compatible: our sites should interface well with screen readers and other assistive technologies; images should have alt-text so those that can't see well can still have an idea of what is being shown.

Comfortable: some people have sensory issues, so we need to make sure we aren't giving people sensory overload; media shouldn't play automatically---it should be opt-in; images shouldn't be too high-saturation

Predictable: if people have a hard time viewing the content of a page, it can be helpful for our blogs to follow common web practices, like having a sidebar or header navbar, or having the content in the center.
